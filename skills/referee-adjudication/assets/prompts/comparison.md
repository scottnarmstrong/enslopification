# Comparison with another draft of the same proof: what to adopt

Read-only, fresh context. Two drafts present the same mathematics:

- **ours:** the frozen snapshot {{packet}};
- **theirs:** {{other_draft}}, written independently.

Compare them for {{reader}}, area by area ({{areas}}). The goal is to find the devices that make theirs easier to
read, and to say precisely where theirs is weaker. **Adopt devices; import no statements.** Ours is checked against
the formal source; theirs is not, so nothing of its mathematics may enter ours.

## Report, in this order

1. **Sources consulted:** which parts of each draft you read in full and which you only sampled, and anything you
   could not open.
2. **Summary of recommendations**, at most ten lines, each one actionable.
3. **For each area:** how theirs and ours are organized (with page counts and the page on which each reaches its
   main theorem); why theirs reads better, if it does; why theirs is worse, if it is; a recommendation: adopt,
   adapt (say how) or keep ours. Name the exact device (for example an "idea of the proof, Step 1...k" paragraph
   with one display per step, interpreting sentences after each definition, labels for each item of a property
   list, a strategy diagram) and where in ours it would go.
4. **Notation and terminology** worth taking, and what to drop from ours.
5. **Where theirs is incomplete or less precise (do not import):** acknowledged gaps, missing proofs, weaker
   statements (a limit taken only along a subsequence, missing derivatives, a narrower class), imprecise
   claims. Give evidence: file and line, or the quoted statement. Where theirs is shorter only because its proofs
   are missing, say so: its form is the lesson, not its page count.
6. **Porting order**, cheapest and highest payoff first.

Print the report as your final answer (do not write files).
